ARC-AGI-3 prioritizes learning efficiency in unfamiliar games through a scoring
metric called **Relative Human Action Efficiency** (RHAE, pronounced "ray").

RHAE measures per-level action efficiency relative to a human baseline. Scores are normalized per game, then averaged across all games.

The procedure can be summarized as follows:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Level score">
    If an agent does not complete a level, its score for that level is 0%. If it completes the level, its action count
    is compared against a human baseline.
    The baseline is the median action count among human finishers. When there is an even number of finishers, the higher of the two middle counts is used.

    The level score is then calculated by dividing the human baseline by the agent's action count, squaring the result, and capping it at 115%.

    For example, if the baseline is 50 actions and the agent takes 100 actions to beat the level,
    its level score is (50 / 100)² = 25%.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Game score">
    Each level is scored independently, and the game score is the weighted average of all level scores.
    Weights correspond to each level's number, so for a five-level game the weights are 1 through 5, summing to 15.
    In this example, the first level's score carries 1/15 of the total weight.

    The game score is then capped at 100% to prioritize generality.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Final benchmark score">
    Average the individual game scores to obtain a final benchmark score ranging from 0% to 100%.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What This Methodology Gives Us

- **Efficiency:** Rewards learning through a small number of highly informative actions and heavily penalizes brute-force trial and error.
- **Human comparison:** Measures action efficiency directly against successful human participants.
- **Limited outlier influence:** The human median reduces sensitivity to exceptional runs; the caps on agent scores reward consistent performance.
- **Room for variation:** Above-human efficiency on some levels can offset modest inefficiency on other levels within the same game.
- **Generality:** The 100% game cap prevents exceptional results in one game from compensating for weaknesses elsewhere. A perfect benchmark score requires completing every level and matching or exceeding human efficiency in every game.
